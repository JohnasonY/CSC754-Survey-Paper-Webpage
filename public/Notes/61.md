# **Kinjo, K., & Tomoeda, A. (2023). Comparison of gap-based and flow-based control strategies using a new controlled stochastic cellular automaton model for traffic flow. arXiv:2308.14291.**

1. Main idea: How can different vehicle control strategies improve traffic flow and reduce congestion? The authors propose a **Controlled Stochastic Optimal Velocity (CSOV) cellular automaton model** to compare two vehicle control strategies in mixed traffic containing human-driven vehicles (HVs) and controlled vehicles (CVs).

2. Proposed CSOV model:

   1. The model extends the Stochastic Optimal Velocity (SOV) model by introducing a control parameter \(\beta\).
   2. Two control strategies are investigated:

      | Strategy | Description |
      |---|---|
      | Gap-based control (GC) | Adjusts vehicle speed to balance the distance to the preceding and following vehicles |
      | Flow-based control (FC) | Adjusts vehicle speed to balance traffic flow rates ahead of and behind the vehicle |

   3. Control strength is determined by \(\beta\):
      - \(\beta=0\): No control (original SOV model).
      - \(\beta=0.1\): Weak control.
      - \(\beta=0.9\): Strong control.

   4. Vehicles move probabilistically according to their calculated velocity, represented as a hopping probability between 0 and 1.

3. Simulation setup:

   1. Single-lane circular road with periodic boundary conditions.
   2. Road length: 200 cells.
   3. Simulation duration: 50,000 time steps.
   4. CAV/CV penetration rates: 10–90% in 10% increments.
   5. Two controlled-vehicle distributions:
      - Uniform placement: Controlled vehicles are evenly distributed.
      - Block placement: Controlled vehicles are grouped together.

4. Results:

   1. **Flow-based control generally improves traffic flow more effectively.**
      - FC consistently increases traffic flow compared with the uncontrolled SOV model under the investigated conditions.
      - GC improves flow in some density ranges but performs worse under weak control at densities between **0.17 and 0.25 vehicles/cell**.
      - FC is more robust to changes in control strength.

   2. **Even low controlled-vehicle penetration improves traffic flow.**
      - At **30% penetration** and weak control (\(\beta=0.1\)), traffic flow improves compared with uncontrolled traffic.
      - Higher penetration generally increases the effectiveness of traffic control.
      - The characteristics of both strategies remain consistent up to approximately 80% penetration.

   3. **Vehicle distribution affects gap-based control.**
      - Block placement extends the metastable traffic state to higher densities.
      - Uniform placement can produce higher flow within the metastable region.
      - FC performance is almost independent of controlled-vehicle placement.

   4. **Flow-based control reduces traffic instability.**
      - GC can produce localized stop-and-go waves and wider velocity distributions.
      - FC maintains more stable vehicle speeds and avoids some congestion patterns observed under GC.
      - At 30% FC penetration, the free-flow density range extends to approximately **0.115 vehicles/cell**, compared with **0.10** for uncontrolled traffic.
      - At 50% penetration, the free-flow range extends to approximately **0.125 vehicles/cell**.

5. Main conclusions:

   1. Flow-based control is generally more effective and robust than gap-based control.
   2. Controlling traffic flow directly can improve throughput and reduce congestion.
   3. Controlled-vehicle penetration and spatial distribution influence traffic performance.
   4. FC provides more stable velocity distributions, potentially reducing fuel consumption.
   5. The model is limited to single-lane circular roads and requires further validation using real-world traffic data.

   **Main contribution:** The paper introduces a stochastic cellular automaton model that directly incorporates flow-based vehicle control and demonstrates that regulating local traffic flow can improve throughput and stability more consistently than regulating vehicle spacing alone.
