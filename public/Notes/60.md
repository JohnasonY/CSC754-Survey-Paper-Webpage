# **Wang, Y., Jiang, Y., Wu, Y., & Yao, Z. (2024). Mitigating traffic oscillation through control of connected automated vehicles: A cellular automata simulation. Expert Systems with Applications, 235, 121275.**

1. Main idea: How can connected automated vehicles (CAVs) reduce stop-and-go traffic oscillations, congestion, and fuel consumption? The authors propose a cellular automaton (CA) model incorporating CAV platooning and an improved **FollowerStopper (FS) control strategy**, allowing CAVs to cooperate rather than operate independently.

2. Proposed model:

   1. Three car-following modes:
      - **HDV:** Human-driven vehicles with reaction delays and random deceleration.
      - **AV:** CAV following an HDV, using the FollowerStopper control strategy.
      - **CAV:** CAV following another CAV, using V2V communication and synchronized platooning.
   2. The FollowerStopper strategy adjusts vehicle speed based on distance and relative velocity to absorb traffic waves.
   3. The proposed strategy extends FS control from individual CAVs to entire CAV platoons.

3. Simulation setup:

   1. Single-lane circular road with periodic boundary conditions.
   2. Road length: 400 m (40,000 cells).
   3. Cell length: 0.01 m.
   4. Vehicle length: 5 m.
   5. Time step: 0.1 s.
   6. Simulation duration: 400 s, analyzing the final 200 s.
   7. Maximum speed: 35 m/s (126 km/h).
   8. CAV penetration rates: 0–100% in 10% increments.
   9. Traffic density: 10–100 vehicles/km.
   10. Each scenario is simulated 20 times with different random seeds.

4. Results:

   1. **CAV platooning improves traffic efficiency.**
      - The proposed strategy increases average vehicle speed compared with conventional FS control.
      - Improvements become more significant as CAV penetration increases.
      - At high traffic densities, conventional FS control becomes less effective, while platoon-based control continues to improve traffic performance.

   2. **Traffic congestion decreases with higher CAV penetration.**
      - The proposed strategy reduces the formation and propagation of stop-and-go traffic waves.
      - Under the studied congested conditions, traffic congestion can be eliminated when CAV penetration reaches approximately **80%**.

   3. **Traffic oscillations are significantly reduced.**
      - Cooperative platooning reduces speed fluctuations and improves traffic stability.
      - Even relatively low CAV penetration can interrupt the propagation of congestion waves.
      - Higher penetration further suppresses traffic oscillations.

   4. **Fuel consumption and emissions decrease.**
      - Compared with conventional FS control, the proposed strategy reduces fuel consumption by up to:
        - **9.8%** at 40 vehicles/km.
        - **20.7%** at 60 vehicles/km.
        - **54.8%** at 80 vehicles/km.
      - At 100 vehicles/km, fuel consumption decreases by **46.2%** at 80% CAV penetration compared with pure HDV traffic.
      - At extremely high densities and low CAV penetration, fuel consumption may initially increase before decreasing as more CAV platoons form.

5. Main conclusions:

   1. Cooperative CAV platooning improves traffic efficiency and reduces congestion.
   2. Platoon-based FollowerStopper control performs better than independent CAV control.
   3. Higher CAV penetration reduces traffic oscillations, fuel consumption, and pollutant emissions.
   4. The proposed strategy remains effective at high traffic densities where conventional FS control becomes less effective.
   5. The model is limited to single-lane traffic and does not consider lane-changing behavior or maximum platoon size.

   **Main contribution:** The paper demonstrates that extending the FollowerStopper control strategy from individual CAVs to coordinated CAV platoons can significantly reduce traffic oscillations, alleviate congestion, and improve energy efficiency in mixed traffic.
