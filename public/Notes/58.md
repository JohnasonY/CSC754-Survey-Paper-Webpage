
# **Zhang, J. X., & Wu, C. X. (2025). Active lane-changing model based on cellular automata to promote CAV platoon. Journal of King Saud University – Computer and Information Sciences, 37, Article 6.**

1. Main idea: How can cellular automata (CA) model cooperative lane-changing and CAV platoon formation to reduce congestion and improve traffic safety? The authors propose an Active Lane-Changing with Platoon Formation (ALC-FP) strategy for mixed traffic containing human-driven vehicles (HDVs), trucks, and connected automated vehicles (CAVs).

2. Proposed model:

   1. Three-lane highway with a temporary closure of the inner lane.
   2. Human-driven cars and trucks follow Gipps-based driving rules.
   3. CAVs operate using ACC or CACC depending on the preceding vehicle.
   4. CAVs actively change lanes to create space for vehicles approaching the closed lane.
   5. Cooperative lane changes encourage CAVs to form platoons with shorter headways.

3. Simulation comparison:

   1. ALC-FP: Active lane-changing with CAV platoon formation.
   2. AW-LC: Conventional accident-warning lane-changing strategy.
   3. Different CAV penetration rates and traffic demand levels are investigated.
   4. Performance is evaluated using vehicle delay, platoon intensity, congestion, and safety indicators.

4. Results:

   1. **Vehicle delay decreases significantly.**
      - At 80% CAV penetration and 1400 vehicles/lane/hour, ALC-FP reduces average delay by **76.36%** compared with AW-LC.

   2. **Traffic safety improves.**
      - At 80% CAV penetration and 2000 vehicles/lane/hour, time exposed to collision risk (TET) decreases from 1653 s to 432 s, a reduction of approximately **73.87%**.

   3. **CAV platooning reduces congestion.**
      - Cooperative lane-changing increases CAV platoon formation.
      - Higher CAV penetration improves traffic stability and reduces congested regions.
      - At 80% CAV penetration, the congestion ratio approaches zero in some simulated scenarios.

   4. **Traffic composition affects performance.**
      - Increasing the proportion of trucks increases delays and reduces platoon intensity.
      - The proposed strategy is less effective at low CAV penetration and high traffic demand.

5. Main conclusions:

   1. Cellular automata can simulate cooperative lane-changing and platoon formation in mixed traffic.
   2. Coordinating CAV lane changes can substantially reduce bottleneck congestion.
   3. Higher CAV penetration improves efficiency and safety.
   4. The model is limited to a three-lane highway bottleneck and assumes reliable V2V communication.

   **Main contribution:** The paper demonstrates how CA-based cooperative lane-changing rules can promote CAV platooning and significantly reduce traffic delay and safety risks at highway bottlenecks.
