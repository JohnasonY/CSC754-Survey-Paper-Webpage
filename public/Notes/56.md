# **Jiang, Y., Wang, S., Yao, Z., Zhao, B., & Wang, Y. (2021). A cellular automata model for mixed traffic flow considering the driving behavior of connected automated vehicle platoons. Physica A, 582, 126262.**

1. Main idea: How do connected automated vehicles (CAVs) and vehicle platooning affect traffic flow, congestion, and road capacity? The authors propose a cellular automata (CA) model that considers human-driven vehicles (HDVs), CAVs, reaction times, and vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication.

2. The model introduces three car-following modes:

   | Mode | Following vehicle | Leading vehicle | Characteristics |
   | ---- | ----------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
   | HDV | Human-driven | HDV or CAV | Longer reaction time and random braking |
   | ACC | CAV | HDV | Shorter reaction time using sensors |
   | CACC | CAV | CAV | V2V communication and synchronized platooning |

   CACC vehicles can maintain smaller safety distances and coordinate acceleration and braking.

3. Simulation setup:

   1. Single-lane circular road with periodic boundary conditions.
   2. Road length: 400 m.
   3. Vehicle length: 5 m.
   4. Time step: 0.1 s.
   5. Maximum speed: 126 km/h.
   6. HDV reaction time: 2 s.
   7. CAV reaction time: 0.6 s.
   8. CACC safety distance: 0.5 m.
   9. CAV penetration rates: 0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100%.

4. Results:

   1. **Road capacity increases significantly with CAV penetration.**
      - At 60% penetration, capacity reaches approximately **1.97 times** the pure HDV capacity.
      - At 80% penetration, capacity reaches approximately **3.24 times** the pure HDV capacity.
      - At 100% penetration, vehicles form stable platoons and maintain maximum speed before density saturation.

   2. **Traffic congestion decreases as CAV penetration increases.**

      At a density of 100 vehicles/km:

      | CAV penetration | Congestion ratio | Congestion reduction |
      | --------------- | ---------------- | -------------------- |
      | 0% | 66.07% | 0% |
      | 20% | 61.92% | 6.28% |
      | 40% | 51.32% | 22.32% |
      | 60% | 39.98% | 39.49% |
      | 80% | 24.22% | 63.36% |
      | 100% | 0% | 100% |

   3. **Vehicle platooning improves traffic stability.**
      - Higher CAV penetration reduces speed fluctuations and stop-and-go traffic.
      - At 80% penetration, velocity fluctuations decrease significantly.
      - At 100% penetration, persistent congestion disappears in the simulated conditions.

   4. **Maximum platoon size affects road capacity.**
      - At low density, platoon size has little effect.
      - At 100 vehicles/km, the optimal maximum platoon size is approximately **4 vehicles**.
      - At 160 vehicles/km, larger platoons continue to improve flow within the tested range.

5. Main conclusions:

   1. CAV platooning can significantly increase traffic capacity and reduce congestion.
   2. Shorter reaction times and smaller safety distances improve traffic efficiency.
   3. Benefits become much more significant at high CAV penetration rates.
   4. The optimal platoon size depends on traffic density.
   5. The model is limited to single-lane traffic without lane-changing behavior.

   **Main contribution:** The paper demonstrates that incorporating CAV platooning and V2V communication into a CA model can substantially improve traffic flow, reduce congestion, and stabilize vehicle movement.
