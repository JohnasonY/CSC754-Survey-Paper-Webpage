
# **Deng, X., Shao, Y., Song, J., & Wu, H. (2022). Traffic flow simulation of modified cellular automata model based on producer-consumer algorithm. PeerJ Computer Science, 8, e1102.**

1. Main idea: How can the Nagel–Schreckenberg (NaSch) cellular automaton model be improved to simulate information sharing between intelligent connected vehicles and reduce traffic congestion? The authors introduce a **producer–consumer algorithm** to replace conventional parallel vehicle updates with random sequential information processing.

2. Proposed improved CA model:

   1. Retains the four basic NaSch rules:
      - Acceleration
      - Deceleration
      - Random slowing
      - Position update
   2. Introduces a multi-buffer producer–consumer mechanism:
      - Vehicle position and speed information are stored in shared buffers.
      - Vehicles access information in a randomly determined order.
      - Vehicle states are updated according to the shared information.
   3. Also investigates driver-dependent acceleration behavior to better represent differences between drivers.

3. Simulation setup:

   1. Single-lane road with open boundary conditions.
   2. Road length: 1000 cells.
   3. Initial number of vehicles: 200.
   4. Initial speed: 1 cell/time step.
   5. Maximum speed: 5 cells/time step.
   6. Random slowing probability: 0.3.
   7. Simulation duration: 1000 steps.
   8. Four models are compared:
      - Original NaSch model.
      - Improved CA model with random updates.
      - NaSch model considering driver factors.
      - Improved CA model considering driver factors.

4. Results:

   1. **Random vehicle updates reduce traffic congestion.**
      - Figure 1 shows fewer and narrower congestion bands in the improved model.
      - Traffic jams dissipate more quickly.
      - Free-flow regions become larger compared with the original NaSch model.

   2. **Flow-density relationships improve.**

      | Model | Maximum flow | Optimal density |
      |---|---|---|
      | Original NaSch | 0.49 | 0.165 |
      | Improved CA | Not explicitly reported | 0.165 |
      | NaSch with driver factors | 0.58 | 0.170 |
      | Improved CA with driver factors | 0.57 | 0.155 |

      - The improved CA model produces higher flow than NaSch at densities below the optimal density.
      - Incorporating driver factors changes the optimal density and overall traffic behavior.
      - The improved model with driver factors has slightly lower peak flow than the corresponding original driver-factor model, despite reducing congestion patterns.

   3. **Vehicle speeds become more stable.**
      - Figure 3 shows fewer stationary vehicles and fewer stop-and-go regions.
      - More vehicles travel at higher speeds.
      - Random updates reduce the persistence of large congestion clusters.

   4. **Driver factors influence congestion formation.**
      - Differences in acceleration behavior affect traffic flow and jam propagation.
      - Combining driver factors with random updates accelerates congestion dissipation and increases free-flow regions.

5. Main conclusions:

   1. The producer–consumer algorithm provides a new approach for modeling information sharing between connected vehicles.
   2. Random sequential updates reduce congestion bands and improve traffic movement compared with conventional NaSch simulations.
   3. Driver-dependent acceleration affects traffic flow and congestion dynamics.
   4. The model is restricted to single-lane traffic and lacks calibration and validation against real-world traffic data.
   5. Further research is needed for multilane traffic, lane changing, weather effects, and mixed manual–automated traffic.

   **Main contribution:** The paper introduces a producer–consumer information-sharing mechanism into the NaSch cellular automaton, demonstrating that random sequential vehicle updates can reduce simulated traffic congestion and increase free-flow regions.
